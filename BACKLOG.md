# Backlog

Ordered roughly by priority. Items marked *(UX polish, 2026-07-28)* were ruled out of scope by the UX polish and accessibility design and are recorded here with its reasoning; *(spec §11)* are the roadmap items deferred from the original design.

1. **Finish the release-date curation.** *(curated dates, 2026-07-30)* Every shoe still sourced `listing` carries a year and nothing more, and every one of them can cross a recency-shortcut boundary, because the listing year runs late and never early (docs/scraping.md §Curated release months). A minority of those still have an indexed review and are cheap; the rest have none, and are budget models, GTX/GTS variants and brands with no English coverage, so no further publisher sitemap will reach them. How many are left, the runbook, the publisher yields and the tested Wayback CDX lead are all docs/operations.md §Resuming release-date curation. Also worth a pass: the curated rows marked `suspect` may have the month off by one — acceptable to ship, cheap to improve if a better source turns up.
2. **Method era: say when a reading is being retired.** *(UX polish, 2026-07-28)* Coverage is era-shaped, and the evidence is in docs/app.md §Coverage: every sparse metric is either arriving or retiring, never uniformly thin. "This test is being retired" is the one thing worth saying about a thin column, and the one thing the dataset cannot currently support — there is no notion of era per test, only the `previousId`/`updateId` links for pairs that were formally superseded (docs/scraping.md §Test lineage). Needs the dataset to carry it. Do not approximate it from the age of the oldest reading; that is precisely what the deleted sparse warning got wrong.
3. **Consider metric help in the column picker.** *(metric help, 2026-08-05)* Every published range metric now has one factual explanation in the reusable registry, but this delivery deliberately stops at the sidebar and Add-filter dialog (docs/app.md §Filters). The column picker remains a dense selection surface with an established direction mark and keyboard path; adding another focus stop to every row may cost more than it clarifies. Test that trade-off on a phone and with a keyboard before reusing the small `?` there, and leave the picker unchanged if the explanation is better discovered where the metric is actually filtered.
4. **Open Graph tags.** *(UX polish, 2026-07-28)* The page has a title and a favicon, so a shared link previews as *something* (docs/app.md §Sharing is copying the address bar), but there are no `og:`/`twitter:` tags and therefore no card. Deferred rather than done because it needs an image, and an image needs a decision about what this tool looks like — a screenshot dates, and there is no logo.
5. **Head-to-head compare view.** *(spec §11)* Select 2–4 shoes for a side-by-side metric comparison.
6. **One home for the categorical disclosures.** *(categorical filters review, 2026-08-02)* `FeaturesFilter` still carries `BrandFilter`'s `summary`, chevron and `li` styling verbatim, including its WHY comments. The segmented duplication that originally shared this item has been removed; what remains is a disclosure primitive only. It is a design task that needs render evidence at both widths and in both themes, not a fix-wave edit. The next change to either disclosure's summary, marker or list treatment is when the duplication stops being deferrable.
